About Anagrams
What is an anagram? ▼

An anagram is a word or phrase formed by rearranging the letters of a different word or phrase, typically using all the original letters exactly once. For example, the word "listen" can be rearranged to form "silent", "enlist", or "tinsel".

Anagrams have been popular throughout history — from ancient Greek puzzles to modern word games like Scrabble and Words with Friends.

What are some famous anagram examples? ▼

Here are some well-known anagram examples:

  • Astronomer → Moon starer
  • Dormitory → Dirty room
  • The eyes → They see
  • Conversation → Voices rant on
  • Slot machines → Cash lost in me
  • Eleven plus two → Twelve plus one
What is the difference between an anagram and a palindrome? ▼

An anagram rearranges the letters of one word to form a completely different word. For example, "cat" → "act".

A palindrome is a word or phrase that reads the same forwards and backwards. For example, "racecar", "level", or "madam".

They are different concepts, though both involve playing with letters in creative ways.

Are anagrams useful for anything practical? ▼

Yes! Anagrams have many practical and creative uses:

  • Word games: Scrabble, Words with Friends, crossword puzzles
  • Education: Vocabulary building and spelling practice
  • Creative writing: Writers use anagrams to create character names and pen names
  • Cryptography: Historically used as a form of simple encryption
  • Brain training: Solving anagrams improves mental flexibility and pattern recognition
